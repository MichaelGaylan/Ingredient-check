# Sourced: revision history

Every major change to Sourced, oldest first. Versions 1.0 to 1.3 were numbered afterwards; the version number has been shown inside the app since 1.4.

## 1.0 (2 Nov 2026): first version
- Scan a barcode and get a 0 to 100 score. Every ingredient claim links to a checked source: peer-reviewed research or a food-safety regulator (EFSA, FDA, WHO, IARC).
- Each finding is graded by how strong the evidence is: Established, Probable, Possible, Preliminary or Reassuring.
- Read an ingredient list from a photo of the label. The text is read on your phone.
- Products are looked up in Open Food Facts, with the US Department of Agriculture (USDA) food database as a backup.
- If a product is missing, you can type its ingredients in and Sourced remembers them for that barcode.

## 1.1 (2 Nov 2026): automatic checks and nutrition
- Ingredients not yet in our library are checked automatically against FDA, WHO and IARC records, as soon as a list is entered.
- Cancer classifications from IARC (the WHO cancer agency) count toward the score.
- A "No concerns found" rating when nothing negative turns up.
- Tap the "not yet reviewed" count to see which ingredients those are.
- Sugar, salt, fat and saturated fat count toward the score, using the UK's "high" levels.

## 1.2 (2 Nov 2026): easier to read, and Settings
- Concerns stand out more and are coloured by how strong the evidence is. EU and US restrictions are shown in bold red.
- Dark mode.
- Settings: choose light, dark or automatic appearance, and a language. Labels in other languages are translated automatically.

## 1.3 (3 Nov 2026): allergens, history and a Home Screen app
- Each not-yet-reviewed ingredient links to a web search.
- Allergen warnings: pick your allergens in Settings (the 14 major US and EU allergens, plus lactose intolerance). Products that contain one get a red warning and the words are highlighted. Allergens never change the score.
- Mark a product "OK for me" (for example, lactose-free milk), or "Keep as allergen", with Undo.
- History of your scans, with favourites and delete.
- A "What a scan can't tell you" section.
- Better-scoring alternatives for low-scoring products.
- Add your own free USDA key in Settings so lookups aren't slowed by the shared limit.
- Install Sourced on your Home Screen. It opens full screen and works offline.

## 1.4 (4 Nov 2026): simpler controls
- One button: "Scan barcode" becomes "Look up barcode" when you type a number.
- Label photo moved to a camera icon inside the ingredient box.
- Help and About in Settings, with the version number.

## 1.5 to 1.8 (4 Nov 2026): tidier findings
- Findings sorted into folder tabs, most serious first.
- Each finding starts as a short three-line summary. Tap to open it; opening one closes the others.
- Bigger, bolder arrow for opening a finding.

## 1.9 to 2.3 (4 Nov 2026): clearer verdicts and allergen controls
- The allergen warning offers lactose as well as milk, and lets you remove an allergen from your list.
- The product name, score ring and verdict take the colour of the most serious finding. A product with an Established concern can't be rated better than "Several concerns".
- "+ Add … to my allergens" buttons sit below the warning, and the warning turns a softer red once added.

## 2.4 (4 Nov 2026): what "permitted" really means
- Ingredients found in FDA or WHO food-additive records are now labelled "Permitted in food" instead of "Reassuring", with the job they do in food (for example, controlling acidity).
- Each one explains that approval covers a food-grade version in small, limited amounts, not the substance in any form or amount, and shows the hazard label for the concentrated chemical where one exists (for example, sulfuric acid: "Causes severe skin burns and eye damage").
- Cancer listings that are about workplace exposure, such as breathing acid mists in factories, are explained and not counted in the score.

## 2.5 (4 Nov 2026): red caveat warnings
- A red warning triangle appears beside an ingredient's rating when its records include a cancer listing or a serious hazard for the pure chemical, even if the ingredient is permitted in food. The rating keeps its own colour.
- Those lines are shown in red in the ingredient's details, and the summary says how many ingredients have a caveat.

## 2.6 (4 Nov 2026): allergen list sources
- Settings → My allergens now says where the list comes from (US FDA and EU labelling law), with links.

## 2.7 (4 Nov 2026): simpler allergen alerts
- Every major allergen now alerts by default. The alert box lists the allergen ingredients the product may contain (for example eggs, whey, oats), and tapping one removes it from your alerts.
- Removed items are listed in Settings → My allergens, each with Undo.
- One "This product is OK for me" button replaces the per-allergen ones. The "+ Add … to my allergens" and "Keep as allergen" buttons are gone, as they are no longer needed.

## 2.8 – 3.2 (4 Nov 2026): shorter allergen alert
- The allergen alert is now a short, light red box with one sentence. The allergen words are links, and tapping one removes it from the list. Changes are managed in Settings → My allergens.
- 2.9: Settings → My allergens shows removed words as a short list; tap one to put it back on the alert.
- 3.0: The "This product is OK for me" button is removed from the alert.
- 3.1: "Not alerting for …" is shown in red so removed allergens are easy to notice.
- 3.2: "Settings → My allergens" in the alert is a link that opens straight to My allergens. The red "Not alerting for …" line also shows under the alert.

## 3.4 (4 Nov 2026): every ingredient shows in Findings
- New "Everyday foods" tab in Findings. Basic foods such as milk, eggs, fish, peanuts and celery used to be skipped silently because there is nothing to check. They are now listed there, with a note that they don't change the score and that any allergens are covered by the allergen alert.

## 3.5 (4 Nov 2026): allergen ticks match the alert
- Removing an allergen's own name from the alert (Milk, Eggs, Soy…) now unticks it in My allergens.
- Removing a single ingredient word (whey, oats) keeps the allergen ticked and shows "except whey" under it, so the list always matches what the alert does.

## 3.6 (4 Nov 2026): US and UK spellings
- Allergen alerts now recognise US and UK spellings and common names, such as mollusks and molluscs, sulfites and sulphites, shellfish, crawfish, soya beans and lupine.

## 3.7 (4 Nov 2026): five new library entries
- New verified entries: sulphites (EFSA 2022), sugar alcohols such as sorbitol and maltitol, liquorice, caffeine (EFSA 2015) and sodium/potassium nitrate (EFSA 2017).
- New "⚠ Label warning" rating for ingredients where the law requires a pack warning at high amounts (sugar alcohols, liquorice, caffeine). These lower the score only slightly and have their own Findings tab.

## 3.8 (4 Nov 2026): our research list
- 45 ingredients we are still researching (for example carmine, TBHQ, sorbates, gums and smoke flavouring) now show in an "Under review" tab in Findings, by name only, with no rating and no effect on the score. The full list is in Settings → Under review, marked as our own ongoing research and not medical or scientific advice. Each moves into the main library once its sources are checked.

## 3.9 (4 Nov 2026): more allergen names
- About 170 more alternative label names now trigger allergen alerts, taken from Food Allergy Research & Education (FARE) lists. Examples: casein hydrolysate and rennet casein (milk), lysozyme E1105 and ovomucoid (egg), farina and matzo (wheat), natto and TVP (soy), arachis oil (peanut), pine nuts and gianduja (tree nuts), langouste and cockles (shellfish), halibut and fish sauce (fish), gomasio and Sesamum indicum (sesame).
- Look-alikes such as milk albumin, coconut milk, water chestnut and butternut squash are excluded.

## 4.0 (4 Nov 2026): cheese and regional dairy names
- Milk alerts now recognise named cheeses (brie, edam, feta, gouda, cheddar, mozzarella, halloumi and more), regional dairy foods (akawi, ashta, ayran, jibna, khoya, labneh, lassi, dahi) and the word "dairy". "Dairy-free", "non-dairy" and vegan cheeses are excluded.

## 4.1 (4 Nov 2026): regional and Latin allergen names
- About 100 more names, including Middle Eastern and South Asian foods (knafeh, kataifi, atta, sooji, laban, jameed, hummus, za'atar), dishes and sauces that contain allergens (aioli, hollandaise, Worcestershire sauce, Caesar dressing, pesto, frangipane), botanical names used on labels (Triticum, Hordeum, Juglans regia, Glycine max) and INS sulphite codes (INS 220–228). Rice vermicelli and sunflower lecithin are excluded.

## 4.2 (4 Nov 2026): tidier Settings
- Removed the empty "Kept as allergen" and "My OK products" lists from Settings. Their buttons were retired in 2.7 and 3.0, so nothing could be added to them any more.

## 4.3 (4 Nov 2026): summary chips jump to Findings
- The chips under the rating (such as "1 × B" or "2 not reviewed") now scroll down and open their matching Findings tab. "Under review" and "not reviewed" have separate chips.

## 4.4 (4 Nov 2026): Findings always shows
- The Findings section is now always shown. When a product has no ingredient list yet, it says so and explains how to add one, instead of disappearing.
- If something goes wrong while building Findings, a short message is shown in its place rather than a blank space.
- Phones that installed Sourced on the Home Screen now check for the latest version each time the app opens.

## 4.5 (4 Nov 2026): About button
- An ⓘ About button at the top of the Home screen opens the About information (sources, privacy, version, revision history) in its own pop-up. The same information is still in Settings → About.

## 4.6 (4 Nov 2026): colour-coded ingredient list
- In "Ingredients checked", every ingredient with a finding is now shown in its Findings colour with its grade letter (A to D, ⚠ label warning, ✓ reassuring or permitted, ? under review), so colour is never the only clue. Your allergens keep their solid red mark.
- Tap a coloured ingredient to jump to it in Findings, already opened.
- A small colour key sits under the list.
- After a label photo, Sourced warns if much of the text looks misread and suggests retaking the photo close up, flat and well lit.

## 4.7 (4 Nov 2026): better label reading
- Label photos are cleaned up before reading: turned to greyscale, contrast boosted, small photos enlarged, and light-on-dark text flipped. This helps with glare, shadows and coloured packs.
- If the first read still looks garbled, Sourced tries again with the original photo and keeps the better result.

## 4.8 (5 Nov 2026): crop label photos
- After you take a label photo, a crop step lets you drag a box over just the ingredient list before it is read. Less text around it means a cleaner read. "Whole photo" skips the crop.

## 4.9 (5 Nov 2026): livelier checking, easier cropping
- While ingredients are being checked, the score circle shows three bouncing dots and a sweeping arc, so it's clear the app is working and not frozen.
- Crop step: every edge now has its own drag handle as well as the corners, the handles are bigger and easier to grab with a finger, and you can drag across the photo to draw a new box.

## 5.0 (5 Nov 2026): smoother crop box on phones
- The crop box no longer fights with the page: while you drag, the screen doesn't scroll or zoom, the photo can't be picked up by mistake, and a second finger is ignored. Resizing is steady on iPhone.

## 5.1 (5 Nov 2026): Explore, and alternatives for your allergens
- New **Explore** tab. Browse by aisle (Meat & Seafood; Dairy, Cheese & Eggs; Baking & Cooking; Bread & Baked Goods; Breakfast; Pantry; Snacks & Sweets; Drinks; Plant-based & Frozen) or search for a product. Results are ranked by Sourced score.
- "Shop at" lets you pick a store (Walmart, Target, Kroger, Costco, Whole Foods, Trader Joe's, Aldi and more) and a market (country). Store lists come from Open Food Facts volunteers, so they are incomplete.
- Products that would set off your allergen alert are hidden in Explore by default, with a switch to show them.
- Alternatives now also appear when a product contains one of your allergens, or is high in sugar, salt or fat. Each one says why it's better (for example "no milk", "not high in sugar, salt or fat"), and none of them set off your allergen alerts.

## 5.2 (5 Nov 2026): more reliable Explore searches
- Explore searches now use Open Food Facts' current search service, with the older one as a backup.
- Searches are spaced out to stay under Open Food Facts' limit of about 10 a minute. If it's busy, Sourced shows a countdown and tries again by itself.
- Results are kept on your phone for a day, so going back to an aisle or search is instant and doesn't count toward the limit.

## 5.3 (5 Nov 2026): many more US products in Explore
- Explore now also searches the USDA FoodData Central branded foods database (about 400,000 US products with manufacturer ingredient lists), alongside Open Food Facts. These products are marked "USDA". USDA has no store information, so they show when "All stores" is picked and the market is United States or Anywhere.
- The Walmart badge is blue, and each store has its own fixed colour from our palette (not the stores' brand colours).

## 5.4 (5 Nov 2026): ready for our own search server
- The app can now use Sourced's own copy of the Open Food Facts database for Explore and alternatives, with no per-minute limit. It switches on once the server is online. Until then, nothing changes.

## 5.5 (5 Nov 2026): product pictures and compare cards
- Products without a photo now show a tidy aisle icon instead of a blank box.
- Tap a product's picture to open it in a small card at the bottom of the screen. Open up to three to compare them side by side. Swipe a card away (or tap ×) to close it, or tap it for the full check. Missing photos (USDA products never have one) are now fetched from Open Food Facts as you scroll, a few at a time, so more products show a picture.

## 5.6 (5 Nov 2026): bigger photos
- Compare-card photos are about twice as big. With more than one card open, slide sideways between them. Swipe a card down (or up) to close it. List thumbnails are a little bigger too.

## 5.7 (5 Nov 2026): one photo at a time
- The photo card now shows one product at a time, large, for matching it on the shelf. Tapping another product's picture replaces it. Swipe it down or tap × to close it.

## Coming before public release

- Privacy policy, and release on Google Play, then the Apple App Store.
