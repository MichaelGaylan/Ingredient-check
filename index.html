About

Where the information comes from

Findings come from our evidence library, where every claim links to a checked source (peer-reviewed papers, EFSA, FDA, WHO, IARC). Ingredients we haven't reviewed are checked automatically against FDA, WHO and IARC records in PubChem. Product data comes from Open Food Facts and USDA FoodData Central. See Method for the scoring formula.

Privacy

Everything you save (history, favourites, allergens, settings) stays on this phone. The app has no account and sends nothing about you anywhere. Barcodes and ingredient names are sent to the public databases above to look them up.

    Every ingredient the scanner knows, with the strongest evidence for each finding. Tap one to see the sources.

    How findings are graded

    Each finding is graded by the strength of the evidence behind it, not by how alarming it sounds. Adapted from the GRADE approach used in clinical guidelines.

    A · EstablishedAn authoritative body (EFSA, FDA, WHO, IARC Group 1) or a systematic review of human studies with consistent results.
    B · ProbableSeveral good human studies point the same way, or a regulator acted on the evidence, but there is no full consensus.
    C · PossibleLimited or mixed human data, a single large observational study, or IARC Group 2B.
    D · PreliminaryCell or animal studies only, often at doses far above normal intake.
    ⚠ Label warningThe law requires a warning on the pack when there is a lot of it (for example sugar alcohols, liquorice, caffeine). Harmful mainly in large amounts.
    ReassuringAn authority reviewed it and found it safe at normal intake, usually with an acceptable daily intake (ADI).
    Not yet reviewedWe have not assessed it. This is not the same as safe.

    How the score works

    The score starts at 100. Each ingredient loses points for its strongest concern: 20 for grade A, 12 for B, 6 for C, 2 for D. When the product database has nutrition data, each of sugars, salt, fat and saturated fat that is “high” by the UK front-of-pack thresholds (per 100 g, or per 100 ml for drinks) costs 10. Reassuring findings and unreviewed ingredients cost nothing. Ingredients not in the library are looked up automatically in FDA, WHO/FAO (JECFA) and IARC records through PubChem: an IARC cancer classification costs points on the same scale (Group 1 as grade A, 2A as B, 2B as C), marked "automatic" in the breakdown, and an FDA or WHO listing shows as reassuring at no cost. Tap the score on any product to see each deduction.

    The score is a summary, not a diagnosis. Two products with the same score can differ in what matters to you, so read the findings.

    How every citation is checked

    Every source was looked up in Crossref or on the regulator's own site before it was added, to confirm it exists and has no retraction. Sources marked source and finding checked also had the specific number or conclusion confirmed in their abstract. Findings show the date they were last reviewed. Product data comes from Open Food Facts, an open database built by volunteers.

    The library is deliberately small. Potassium bromate, propylparaben, TBHQ and azodicarbonamide are next, and will appear once their sources pass the same check.
